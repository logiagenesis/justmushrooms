    Lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus) is a unique mushroom that typically grows on dead hardwood trees like oak. It has a rich history in East Asian medicine and is increasingly recognized for its potential cognitive benefits. Research suggests that lion's mane may promote nerve development and protect against nerve damage, making it a subject of interest for its nootropic properties.

    Many people are turning to lion's mane for its potential to enhance cognition, particularly in relation to conditions such as Alzheimer's disease, dementia, and various gastrointestinal issues. One of its key compounds stimulates the production of nerve growth factor (NGF), which supports the growth of brain cells and may improve memory and focus.

    Recent studies indicate that lion's mane could be beneficial for individuals with ADHD and other concentration difficulties. An older Japanese study involving adults aged 50 to 80 with mild cognitive impairment found that those who consumed lion's mane extract daily for 16 weeks exhibited significant improvements in cognitive function compared to a placebo group. As interest in lion's mane continues to grow, its role in supporting brain health is becoming more widely acknowledged.
