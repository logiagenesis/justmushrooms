# 24 — Storefront copy rewrite (4 September 2026)

This shop was written like a regulatory brief. The homepage sold caution. That is the opposite of what Takealot, Woolworths, Faithful to Nature, and the global mushroom brands do.

## What was wrong

The rebuild voice rules said: describe the object, not the outcome; name the limits in the same breath as the finding; when a rule and a sale conflict, the rule wins. The theme then made `what_it_does_not_do` a required, above-the-fold column.

Visible result on the Pages demo:

- Hero lede: "what the evidence says, and what it does not"
- Trust chip: "No cures, no miracles"
- Species intro: "what it does not do, and who should be cautious"
- Homepage H2: "What mushrooms may support, and what they do not do"
- A four-bullet list naming cancer, dementia, depression, anxiety, diabetes
- Every PDP repeating "What this product does not do"
- Product promises that were parts lists

A customer arrived to buy a bottle and was handed reasons not to.

The SAHPRA line is still required. It belongs in the footer and in one collapsed notice. It does not belong in the H1.

## What the best shops actually do

### South African retail

Takealot, Checkers Sixty60, Woolworths, Makro, Bash, Superbalist, Mr Price, Clicks, Dis-Chem, Faithful to Nature.

Pattern: desire in the heading, proof underneath, policy in the footer. Woolworths sells quality and ritual. Faithful to Nature sells "why we love it" and "support your daily well-being", then ingredients, then a care line. Clicks and Dis-Chem sell the benefit on the pack shot and keep the complementary-medicine notice at the bottom of the tab. Nobody opens a category page with a list of diseases the SKU does not treat.

### South African mushroom shelves

Faithful to Nature, Medi Mushrooms, Harmonic Mycology, Aether, African Forest Medicinals, Sfera, Soaring Free, Wellness Warehouse listings.

Pattern: vitality, daily well-being, origin story (Knysna forest, family bench, Cape harvest), "why you'll reach for it". Legal language exists. It is not the merchandising.

### Global mushroom stores

Real Mushrooms, FreshCap, Host Defense, Four Sigmatic, Life Cykel, Rainbo, North Spore, Mushroom Revival, Bristol Fungarium.

Pattern: identity + ritual + craft + one proof point. Real Mushrooms sells fruiting-body purity. FreshCap sells "this mushroom for this part of your day". Four Sigmatic sells the morning ritual. Host Defense sells mythos. Life Cykel and Rainbo sell the lifestyle dropper. Disclaimer at the edge of the page.

## What we changed

Voice rules in `docs/09-content-drafts.md` now say the homepage sells the ritual and the footer carries the law.

Rewritten in this pass:

- Homepage hero, trust strip, species band, finder, featured products, education split
- About, FAQ, sourcing
- Product promises, evidence summaries, SEO for all 23 SKUs
- Species short descriptions and SEO
- PDP "does not do" column collapsed into a complementary-product notice
- Species evidence headings
- Newsletter, announcement, editorial CTA cards

Not rewritten: the cited evidence matrices, the safety notes for Reishi (liver), Chaga (kidney stones) and Sceletium (antidepressants). Those protect the customer. They stay in warnings.

## The line we will not cross

No disease treatment claims. No "clinically proven". No "boosts immunity". No transferring PSK evidence onto a tincture. Complementary product, SAHPRA sentence, alcohol notice, pet disclaimer — all still on the page.

The genius is placement, not deletion.
