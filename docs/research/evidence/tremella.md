# Tremella / Snow Fungus (Tremella fuciformis) — Evidence Matrix

**Product context:** Just Mushrooms ~30% ethanol fruit-body tincture, label "±1000 mg per 1 ml equivalent", sold in South Africa as a health supplement under SAHPRA jurisdiction [19][20].

**Research-method note.** Compiled 2026-09-02 from PubMed/PMC abstracts, GBIF, Frontiers, Springer, ScienceDirect and Wikipedia (identity/fun facts only). The single human RCT (Ban 2018) was read from the publisher abstract. Figures from secondary sources are flagged "(secondary)". Unverifiable items are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

**Overall grade: Limited human evidence** for subjective memory complaints (one 8-week RCT, n = 75) and for topical skin hydration; **Traditional use** for the classic "lung/yin tonic" and beauty uses; all other actions are **Animal/in-vitro evidence**.

## 2. Traditional and culinary uses

- Known in China as *yiner*/*baimuer* and "used as a Chinese herb for many years" and "for health promotion and longevity" [4][5]. — **Traditional use**
- Chinese medicine classes it as a sweet, neutral yin tonic that "moistens the Lungs and Stomach", used for dry cough, dry throat, thirst after illness and weakness; Qing-dynasty materia medica (*Ben Cao Zai Xin*, *Ben Cao Wen Da*) are the first texts to document it as a distinct medicinal — details from a TCM reference site (secondary; verify against primary materia medica) [17]. — **Traditional use**
- Historically a beauty/complexion food ("commoner's bird's nest") [17]; ScienceDirect repeats the legend of the Tang concubine Yang Guifei using it for her complexion — legend, not evidence [5].
- Culinary: rehydrated and simmered into sweet soups with jujube, lotus seed or rock sugar; prized for its crunchy-gelatinous texture rather than flavour [3][17].

**Systematic reviews / authoritative summaries.** No Cochrane review, MSKCC monograph or EFSA opinion on *T. fuciformis* was located (**UNVERIFIED absence**). Narrative reviews (Ma 2018; 2025–2026 polysaccharide reviews; Mineroff & Jagdeo 2023 for skin) all conclude that human data are scarce and further clinical research is needed [4][7][8][13].

**Summary.** One small, short RCT supports a modest effect on subjective memory complaints; the purified-polysaccharide drug used in China is a different product (isolated TFPS, enteric-coated, prescription) from a whole-fruit-body ethanol tincture. Skin evidence in humans is topical only. **Grade: Limited human evidence** (cognition, topical skin); everything else **Animal/in-vitro** or **Traditional use**.

## 5. Animal / in-vitro evidence summary

- **Immune / inflammation:** TFPS activated macrophages and modulated cytokines in vitro; in mice, TFPS relieved induced atopic dermatitis via immune and gut-microbiota changes [8][10].
- **Skin / ageing:** in D-galactose-aged mice, TFPS raised skin hydroxyproline and hyaluronic acid, increased SOD/GSH-Px and lowered MDA, IL-1β and TNF-α; TFPS protected cultured human dermal fibroblasts from UVA damage (Nrf2/Keap1) [11][13].
- **Glucose / lipids:** TFPS lowered blood glucose and triglycerides in diabetic-model mice with gut-microbiome shifts [12]; older work with acidic polysaccharides from *Tremella* spp. showed hypoglycaemic effects in mice [8].
- **Neuro:** TFPS improved memory in rats and promoted neurite outgrowth in PC12 cells (cited within the Ban 2018 trial) [6]; reviews describe "neuroprotective" and "memory-enhancing" activity in rodents [8].
- **Antioxidant / anti-tumour:** in-vitro radical scavenging and cytotoxicity data [8][22].
- Early Chinese pharmacology (1980s) reported effects in rats [18].
- None of these has been confirmed in a human trial.

- "Rich in naturally water-holding polysaccharides that are studied for skin hydration in cosmetic formulations." — **Limited human evidence** (topical) / **Animal/in-vitro** (oral) [13][14]
- "A small clinical study has explored tremella and subjective memory in adults." — **Limited human evidence** [6]
- "Studied in the laboratory for antioxidant and immune-modulating activity." — **Animal/in-vitro evidence** [8][10]
- Oral "skin from within", "collagen", "anti-ageing", blood-sugar, cholesterol, lung or immune claims in people — **Unverified — do not publish**

## 7. What tremella does NOT do / cannot be claimed

- Is **not** a treatment for dementia, Alzheimer's disease or "brain fog"; the only RCT was 8 weeks in people with *subjective* complaints, not diagnosed impairment [6]. Disease claims are prohibited for health supplements [19].
- Is **not** a treatment for cancer, chemotherapy side-effects, hepatitis or low white-cell counts — that use belongs to a Chinese prescription drug made from purified TFPS, not to a food-grade tincture [4].
- Is **not** "oral hyaluronic acid" or a "collagen booster"; human skin data are topical [13][14].
- Does **not** treat diabetes, high cholesterol, asthma, cough, constipation, depression, anxiety or ADHD — no human trials [8].
- No claim of "clinically proven", "guaranteed" or "doctor-recommended" results.
- The "1000 mg per 1 ml equivalent" figure is **UNVERIFIED** and must be substantiated by the extraction ratio; because TFPS precipitates in ethanol, an ethanol tincture cannot be assumed to deliver the polysaccharide doses used in research [7][9].

## 8. Safety

- **Adverse events in trials:** in Ban 2018 AE rates did not differ from placebo (≈35–41% in all arms, nature not specified in abstract) at up to 1,200 mg/day for 8 weeks [6]. Reviews state no significant adverse effects are known for consumption [21]; long-term supplement safety data are lacking.
- **Allergy / case reports:** no published allergic or toxic case report specific to *T. fuciformis* was located — **UNVERIFIED absence**; as with any fungus, mushroom-allergic individuals should avoid it.
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding:** no human data located in any source — **UNVERIFIED**; label "not recommended unless advised by a healthcare professional".
- **Medication interactions (theoretical, preclinical only):** glucose- and lipid-lowering in rodents [12] → caution with insulin/antidiabetics; immunomodulation [8][10] → caution with immunosuppressants; no antiplatelet data found for *T. fuciformis* itself (**UNVERIFIED**). No human interaction study exists.
- **Ethanol-tincture caveats:** ~30% v/v ethanol — unsuitable for children, pregnancy, alcohol dependence/recovery, disulfiram or metronidazole users; declare alcohol content. Ethanol also precipitates the very polysaccharides the research concerns, so the tincture's composition differs from the hot-water decoctions and purified TFPS used in studies [7][9].
- **Quality:** raw material provenance matters; over 90% of world supply comes from Gutian County, Fujian [15][16].

## 9. Approved claim language (draft, legally cautious)

Tremella, or snow fungus (*Tremella fuciformis*), is a jelly mushroom with a long history in Chinese cooking, where it is simmered into nourishing soups and traditionally regarded as a gentle "yin" tonic. Our tincture is prepared from the cultivated fruit body. Tremella is naturally rich in water-holding polysaccharides, which are widely studied in the laboratory and used in skincare formulations for hydration. One small clinical study has begun to explore tremella and everyday memory in adults; research is at an early stage. This product is a health supplement, not a medicine, and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Do not use if you are allergic to mushrooms, pregnant or breastfeeding, or without medical advice if you take diabetes or immune-suppressing medication. Contains approximately 30% alcohol.

## 11. FAQ

**Q1. What is snow fungus?** A gelatinous white jelly fungus, *Tremella fuciformis* Berk., that grows on dead hardwood by parasitising another fungus (*Annulohypoxylon archeri*); it has been cultivated in China since the 19th century [1][3].

**Q2. Is there any human research?** One randomised, double-blind trial in 75 Korean adults with subjective memory complaints found improvements in memory questionnaires and short-term memory after 8 weeks of 600–1,200 mg/day, with adverse events similar to placebo [6]. That is the only oral RCT located.

**Q3. Does tremella hydrate skin?** Its polysaccharides hold water and improved skin moisture when applied topically in a small formulation study [13][14]; in mice, oral TFPS improved skin-ageing markers [11]. There is no human trial of oral tremella for skin — **UNVERIFIED**.

**Q4. Is it safe?** It is a widely eaten food; the one RCT reported adverse-event rates no different from placebo [6] and reviews report no known significant adverse effects [21]. No pregnancy or breastfeeding data exist.

**Q5. Is it the same as the Chinese "Tremella polysaccharide capsules"?** No. Those are a purified-polysaccharide prescription drug approved in China in 2002 for chemotherapy-related leukopenia and as an adjuvant in hepatitis [4]; a food-grade ethanol tincture is a different product and cannot borrow those claims.

**Q6. Why does the ethanol matter?** Tremella's active fraction is water-soluble polysaccharide, which is normally *precipitated* by ethanol during extraction [7][9]. A 30% ethanol tincture therefore contains a different, and unquantified, profile from the hot-water preparations used in research.

## 13. Verified fun facts

1. Snow fungus was first described scientifically in 1856 by the English mycologist Miles Joseph Berkeley — from specimens collected in Brazil by the explorer Richard Spruce, not from China [1][3].
2. It cannot grow on its own: it is a mycoparasite that only forms its white fronds after invading the fungus *Annulohypoxylon archeri*, so commercial growers must inoculate logs or sawdust with both species at once ("dual culture") [3].
3. A single county — Gutian in Fujian, China — produced over 385,000 tonnes of tremella in 2022, more than 90% of the world's supply [16].
