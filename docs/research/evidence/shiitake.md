# Shiitake (Lentinula edodes) — Evidence Matrix

**Product context:** Shiitake appears in the Just Mushrooms **"Myco-Radiance" skin blend** (30% ethanol tincture, approx. 1000 mg/ml dried-material equivalent). Sold in South Africa as a Category D complementary medicine / health supplement under SAHPRA jurisdiction [20][21]. The client's skin copy claims shiitake delivers "kojic acid [that] brightens skin", supports "collagen production", and helps "reduce pigmentation" — these are assessed in §6–§7 and are largely **not supportable for an oral tincture**.

**Research-method note.** Compiled 2026-09-02. The MSK About Herbs shiitake monograph and key papers were retrieved server-side; figures from search summaries are flagged "(summary — verify)". Unconfirmed items are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

**Overall grade:** **Limited human evidence** for immune-marker changes from daily *eating* of whole shiitake (Dai 2015) and for lipid/microbiota effects; **Traditional use** as food and tonic. The skin-brightening / collagen / anti-pigmentation claims for an oral extract are **Unverified — do not publish**.

## 2. Traditional and culinary uses

- Long culinary and medicinal history in East Asia (China, Japan); eaten as food and used as a tonic and "anticancer" agent in traditional practice [2].
- Traditional record centres on general vitality and, in modern Japan, on lentinan-based cancer adjuvant therapy (a pharmaceutical, not a food claim — see §3) [2][7].

**Summary.** The strongest consumer-relevant human data (Dai 2015) is for **eating whole cooked shiitake**, shows *immune-marker* changes (not disease outcomes), and does not transfer to a 30% ethanol tincture at label dose. Lentinan cancer data are for an **injectable/oral pharmaceutical**, not a supplement claim. **No human skin/brightening/collagen study of oral shiitake exists** (see §6).

## 5. Animal / in-vitro evidence

- Preclinical: immunostimulatory, antiviral (poliovirus, bovine herpesvirus; a low-MW lignin inhibits hepatitis C), antibacterial (Th1/macrophage activation), hepatoprotective, antihypercholesterolaemic (eritadenine ↑ CYP7A1), antiproliferative, cytotoxic (HepG2 apoptosis via caspase-3/-8), antimutagenic, and anticaries [2].
- Eritadenine + shiitake reduced hepatic SAH-hydrolase and restored DNA-methylase expression in homocysteinaemic mice [5].
- **Skin (in-vitro only):** a 2025 study of a **75% ethanol shiitake extract** (a topical/cosmetic context) showed moderate antioxidant capacity, ~52% elastase inhibition and concentration-dependent collagenase inhibition, and was proposed for **topical anti-aging creams** — an in-vitro/cosmeceutical finding, **not** evidence that an oral tincture brightens skin or builds collagen [15].
- None reproduced as a confirmed oral-supplement skin effect in humans.

## 6. What shiitake MAY support (tiered, compliant phrasing)

- **Limited human evidence:** "Eating shiitake mushrooms daily was associated with changes in immune-cell activity in a small study" — must be framed as *food*, not this tincture, and not as disease prevention [12].
- **Traditional use:** "A culinary and traditional-medicine mushroom of East Asia." [2]
- **Animal/in-vitro:** "Contains eritadenine and β-glucans studied in the laboratory." (compositional) [5][10]
- **Unverified — do not publish (the client's skin claims):**
  - "Kojic acid brightens skin" — kojic acid is an *Aspergillus* fermentation product, not a characteristic shiitake compound; no evidence an oral shiitake tincture delivers a meaningful kojic-acid dose or brightens skin [16][17].
  - "Collagen production" / "reduce pigmentation" — only in-vitro/topical anti-elastase/anti-collagenase and tyrosinase data exist (75% ethanol extract, cosmetic context); **no oral human skin outcome** [15].

## 7. What shiitake does NOT do / cannot be claimed (esp. for the skin blend)

- Does **not** have human evidence that *oral* shiitake brightens skin, reduces pigmentation, or increases collagen. The only skin data are in-vitro/topical [15].
- "Kojic acid" is **not** a legitimate shiitake selling point — attribute kojic acid to koji/*Aspergillus*, and note that even topical kojic acid's brightening is a cosmetic-ingredient claim, not an oral-supplement one [16][17].
- **Lentinan** benefits **cannot** be claimed for a tincture — the evidence is for an injectable/oral cancer drug, and disease claims are prohibited for Category D supplements [2][7][20].
- Does **not** have proven cholesterol-lowering effect in people from a tincture — human lipid data are for shiitake *bars/extracts* and remain limited; MSK states there is "no proof from clinical trials" that shiitake lowers cholesterol in people [2].
- No cancer treatment/prevention claim (prostate-cancer trial was negative; disease claims prohibited) [2][20].

## 8. Safety

- **Shiitake (flagellate) dermatitis.** A well-documented toxicoderma: 24–48 h after eating **raw or undercooked** shiitake, patients develop intensely itchy, whiplash-like **linear erythematous streaks** (flagellate pattern) on trunk and limbs, arranged in a Köbner (scratch) pattern. First described by Nakamura (Japan, 1977); ~100+ cases reported, increasingly in the West. The thermolabile polysaccharide **lentinan** is the leading suspect (IL-1 secretion → vasodilation/haemorrhage), possibly with an allergic component; adequate cooking denatures it [13][14]. Relevant caution: an extract could in principle carry this risk in sensitive individuals — **UNVERIFIED for tinctures specifically**.
- **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis** from inhaling shiitake **spores** (cultivation/handling exposure) — case reports [2].
- **Photosensitivity, eosinophilia, dermatitis and GI upset** after prolonged consumption of **shiitake powder**; chronic consumption may raise eosinophil counts [2].
- **Food allergy** (including oesophageal symptoms) and, from eating whole/large pieces, rare **small-bowel obstruction** — case reports (not tincture-relevant) [2].
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding.** No specific supplement safety data — advise caution (**UNVERIFIED / no data**).
- **Ethanol-tincture caveats.** 30% v/v ethanol: unsuitable for children, pregnant/breastfeeding women, alcohol-use disorder, liver disease, and those on disulfiram/metronidazole; declare alcohol content. SA-specific alcohol labelling: check SAHPRA guidance [20].

2. Shiitake naturally contains β-glucans, eritadenine, ergothioneine and B-vitamins.
3. Shiitake is one of the world's most widely eaten mushrooms and has a long history of traditional use.
4. This is a health supplement and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
5. Contains 30% alcohol by volume; not for children, or during pregnancy or breastfeeding.
6. If you have a known mushroom allergy, consult a healthcare practitioner before use.

## 10. Disallowed claim language

- "Kojic acid brightens skin / fades dark spots" (kojic acid is not a characteristic shiitake compound; no oral evidence) [16][17]
- "Boosts collagen production", "reduces pigmentation", "for radiant skin" (in-vitro/topical only) [15]
- "Contains lentinan that treats/prevents cancer" (injectable drug; disease claim) [2][7][20]
- "Lowers cholesterol" (no clinical proof for this product) [2]
- "Immune booster" as disease prevention [20]
- "Anti-viral / anti-bacterial / anti-fungal" as consumer claims (preclinical only) [2]
- "Clinically proven", "doctor recommended"

## 11. FAQ

**Q1. Does shiitake brighten skin?** There is no human evidence that taking shiitake by mouth brightens skin or reduces pigmentation. Laboratory and topical-cosmetic studies show some tyrosinase/collagenase/elastase effects, but that is not proof for an oral tincture [15][16][17].

**Q2. Does shiitake contain kojic acid?** Kojic acid is mainly a fermentation product of the koji mould *Aspergillus oryzae*, not a characteristic compound of shiitake. Shiitake is not an established dietary source of kojic acid [16][17].

**Q3. What is lentinan?** A β-glucan from shiitake that in Japan is an **injectable prescription drug** used alongside chemotherapy for stomach cancer (approved 1985). Its benefits are for the injected/oral drug, not for a food supplement [2][7][8].

**Q4. Is there any human evidence for shiitake?** Yes, but limited: a 52-person study found that **eating** 5–10 g of dried shiitake daily for 4 weeks changed immune-cell markers (Dai 2015). It measured lab markers, not disease outcomes [12].

**Q5. Can raw shiitake cause a rash?** Yes — "shiitake flagellate dermatitis," an itchy whiplash-pattern rash 1–2 days after eating raw/undercooked shiitake, linked to lentinan; cooking prevents it [13][14].

**Q6. Does shiitake lower cholesterol?** Shiitake's eritadenine lowers cholesterol in animals, and small human studies with shiitake bars/extracts are suggestive, but MSK notes there is no clinical proof it lowers cholesterol in people — and none for this tincture [2][5].

## 13. Fun facts (cited)

1. Shiitake's cancer drug is a needle, not a capsule: **lentinan** has been an approved **injectable** adjuvant for stomach cancer in Japan since **1985**, which is exactly why "lentinan" cannot be used to sell a mushroom tincture [7][8].
2. Eating shiitake can whip up a rash shaped like whip-marks — **flagellate dermatitis**, first described by Nakamura in **1977**, appears as linear whiplash streaks 1–2 days after eating raw or undercooked shiitake, and thorough cooking prevents it [13][14].
3. The "kojic acid" beloved of skin-brightening serums is named for **koji** — the *Aspergillus oryzae* mould used to make sake, miso and soy sauce — and was first isolated from that mould by Saito in **1907**, not from any mushroom [16][17].
