# Cordyceps (Cordyceps militaris & Ophiocordyceps sinensis) — Evidence Matrix

**Product context:** Just Mushrooms 30% ethanol tincture, approx. 1000 mg/ml dried-material equivalent. Sold in South Africa as a complementary medicine / health supplement (Category D) under SAHPRA jurisdiction [24][25]. The client's current label reads **"Ophiocordyceps militaris"** — a binomial that does not exist (see §1). This matrix recommends re-labelling to **"Cordyceps militaris"** pending client confirmation of the actual raw material.

**Research-method note.** Compiled 2026-09-02. Academic full-text (PubMed/PMC/Cochrane) was reached through a server-side research tool and the MSK About Herbs monograph was retrieved in full. Statements drawn from search-result summaries rather than full text are flagged "(summary — verify)". Items that could not be confirmed are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

**Overall grade:** **Limited human evidence** for exercise/aerobic capacity (mixed, small trials, mostly cultivated/fermented product) with **Traditional use** for tonic/fatigue indications. No indication reaches "Strong human evidence".

## 1. Identity

| Field | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The label problem | "**Ophiocordyceps militaris**" is not a valid name. *Ophiocordyceps* (family Ophiocordycipitaceae) and *Cordyceps* (family Cordycipitaceae) were split in the 2007 multigene reclassification; *militaris* is the **type species of the genus *Cordyceps*** and stayed in *Cordyceps*. The species is *Cordyceps militaris*; there is no *Ophiocordyceps militaris*. | [4][6] |

Mostly sold as fermented **mycelium "Cs-4"** (strain *Paecilomyces / Samsoniella hepiali*), not wild fruiting body | [3][6][7] |

**Recommendation to client:** change label binomial to **"Cordyceps militaris"**. If the raw material is genuinely wild *O. sinensis* or a Cs-4 mycelial ferment, label it "*Ophiocordyceps sinensis* (Cs-4 fermented mycelium)". Do not use "Ophiocordyceps militaris" on any copy — it is taxonomically impossible and an easy target for a regulator or competitor.

## 2. Traditional uses

- *O. sinensis* has ~2,000 years of use in traditional Chinese and Tibetan medicine; first written records are cited to around 200 AD, and it was historically reserved for imperial/noble use because of its rarity [7] (summary — verify the 200 AD date before publishing).
- Traditional indications: fatigue, weakness after illness, ageing, "kidney" and "lung" tonic, cough, and sexual dysfunction ("Himalayan Viagra") [3][6].
- MSK records traditional use "for a wide range of conditions including fatigue, sexual dysfunction, coughs, and as an adaptogen or immune stimulant" [3].
- *C. militaris* is a separate, historically minor "chong cao" used interchangeably in later Chinese practice; most of the traditional record is for *O. sinensis* [6].

**Key formulation point:** cordycepin is essentially a *C. militaris* story; if the client's product is genuinely *militaris*, cordycepin content is a legitimate compositional talking point. If it is Cs-4 *sinensis* mycelium, cordycepin claims are largely unsupportable.

**Renal / other clinical claims (see §7):** Several small Chinese trials report *O. sinensis* improved renal function in transplant or nephropathy patients [3], but two independent analyses — including a **Cochrane review (Hong 2015)** on *C. sinensis* for kidney-transplant recipients and a 2019 meta-analysis on haemodialysis patients — concluded the evidence is **insufficient/low-quality** [3, refs 26–27]. Not a basis for consumer claims.

**Summary.** Human data are limited, small, and mostly on Cs-4 (*sinensis*) or multi-mushroom blends, with one clear null (Parcell 2004) and two positives (Chen 2010, older adults; Hirsch 2017, chronic dosing). LiverTox notes cordyceps clinical research overall is "limited and too low in quality to identify any therapeutic potential" [8]. No result justifies "Strong human evidence" and none is on a single-species *C. militaris* tincture at label dose.

## 5. Animal / in-vitro evidence

- MSK: preclinical antitumor, radioprotective, antiplatelet, antidiabetic effects; enhanced recovery from taxol-induced leukopenia in mice; increased cisplatin cytotoxicity in NSCLC cells [3].
- Mechanistic: stimulates T-helper cells, prolongs lymphocyte survival, raises TNF-α/IL-1, increases NK-cell activity in lab studies; cordycepin inhibits collagen-induced platelet aggregation via cAMP/cGMP and lowered thromboxane A2 [3].
- Cordyceps stimulated testosterone/steroidogenesis in mouse Leydig cells; not shown in humans — an 8-week human study of *C. sinensis* did **not** change testosterone [3][11].
- None reproduced as a confirmed clinical effect in humans.

## 6. What cordyceps MAY support (tiered, compliant phrasing)

- **Limited human evidence:** "May support exercise capacity / aerobic performance with regular use" — supported only by Chen 2010 (older adults, Cs-4) and Hirsch 2017 (chronic, blend); phrase cautiously and note it is not established for a single-species tincture [14][15].
- **Traditional use:** "Traditionally used as a tonic for energy and after illness in Chinese medicine." [3][6]
- **Animal/in-vitro:** "Contains cordycepin and polysaccharides studied in laboratory research." (compositional, *militaris* only for cordycepin) [10][11]
- **Unverified — do not publish:** any human claim for immunity, kidney function, libido, blood sugar, or cancer.

## 7. What cordyceps does NOT do / cannot be claimed

- **Not for diabetes / blood sugar control** — antidiabetic effects are rodent/in-vitro only; MSK lists additive hypoglycaemia as a theoretical *interaction risk*, not a benefit [3].
- **Not for atherosclerosis / heart disease** — no controlled human outcome evidence.
- **Not for sexual dysfunction** — traditional "Himalayan Viagra" reputation and mouse testosterone data only; a human study showed no testosterone change [3][11].
- **Not for kidney disease** — Cochrane and a meta-analysis found evidence insufficient; do not imply cordyceps "supports kidney health" [3].
- **Not for cancer** — antitumor data are preclinical; MSK notes clinical antitumor evidence is lacking. Disease claims are prohibited for Category D health supplements under SAHPRA [24][25].

## 8. Safety

- **Bleeding / anticoagulant interaction.** Cordycepin inhibits platelet aggregation in vitro; MSK lists a **case report of excessive post-tooth-extraction bleeding** in a daily cordyceps user, and flags additive risk with warfarin/antiplatelets [3]. Advise caution before surgery and with blood thinners.
- **Immunosuppressant / cyclosporine interaction.** Cordyceps is immunostimulatory (raises NK activity, T-helper cells); it may theoretically oppose immunosuppressant therapy. Conversely, anecdotal data suggest it *reduced* cyclosporin- and aminoglycoside-induced renal toxicity in transplant patients [3] — a mixed, unresolved interaction. Anyone on cyclosporine or other immunosuppressants (transplant recipients) should not use it without medical supervision.
- **Hypoglycaemia.** Additive with insulin/antidiabetic drugs (theoretical, lab-based) — caution [3].
- **Myelogenous cancers.** MSK advises against use with myelogenous-type cancers because cordyceps proliferated erythroid progenitor cells in mice [3].
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding.** No human safety data — advise avoidance (**UNVERIFIED / no data**).
- **Allergy.** Fungal allergy possible; standard "discontinue if reaction" advice.
- **Ethanol-tincture caveats.** A 30% v/v ethanol preparation is unsuitable for children, pregnant/breastfeeding women, people with alcohol-use disorder or liver disease, and those on disulfiram/metronidazole; declare the alcohol content. Specific SA labelling rules for alcohol: check SAHPRA labelling guidance [24].

## 9. Approved claim language (draft, legally cautious)

1. Just Mushrooms Cordyceps is a 30% ethanol extract of cultivated *Cordyceps militaris*.
2. Cordyceps has a long history of traditional use in Chinese medicine as a tonic for energy and vitality.
3. *Cordyceps militaris* naturally contains cordycepin (3′-deoxyadenosine), adenosine and polysaccharides.
4. Small human studies have explored cordyceps and exercise capacity with mixed results; benefits are not established for this product.
5. This is a health supplement and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
6. Contains 30% alcohol by volume; not for children, or during pregnancy or breastfeeding.
7. If you take blood thinners, diabetes medicines, or immune-suppressing medicines, or are due for surgery, consult a healthcare practitioner before use.

## 10. Disallowed claim language

- "Ophiocordyceps militaris" (non-existent binomial) [4][6]
- "Boosts testosterone", "natural Viagra", "improves sexual performance" [3][11]
- "Controls/treats diabetes", "balances blood sugar" [3]
- "Supports kidney health", "protects the kidneys" [3]
- "Anti-cancer", "fights tumours" [3][24]
- "Clinically proven to boost energy / VO₂max" (single-species tincture unproven) [8][13]
- "Immune booster" as a disease-prevention claim [24]
- "Rich in cordycepin" if the product is actually Cs-4 *sinensis* mycelium [11]

## 11. FAQ

**Q1. Is "Ophiocordyceps militaris" a real species?** No. The species is *Cordyceps militaris*. *Militaris* is the type species of *Cordyceps* and was never moved to *Ophiocordyceps* in the 2007 reclassification; only *sinensis* and relatives moved [4][6].

**Q2. What is the difference between the two cordyceps?** *C. militaris* is cultivated, affordable, and rich in cordycepin; *O. sinensis* is the wild caterpillar fungus, extremely expensive, and usually sold as fermented "Cs-4" mycelium that makes little cordycepin [6][11].

**Q3. Does cordyceps improve athletic performance?** The human trials are small and mixed: null in trained cyclists (Parcell 2004), positive in older adults (Chen 2010) and after 3 weeks of a mushroom blend (Hirsch 2017). It is not proven for a single-species tincture [13][14][15].

**Q4. What is cordycepin?** A nucleoside (3′-deoxyadenosine) that is the most-studied cordyceps compound; it is found mainly in *C. militaris* [10][11].

**Q5. Is cordyceps safe with my medicines?** Use caution with blood thinners (bleeding case report), diabetes medicines, and immune-suppressing drugs such as cyclosporine; ask a healthcare practitioner [3].

**Q6. Why is the "zombie fungus" a cordyceps?** *Cordyceps* and the related *Ophiocordyceps* are insect-parasitising fungi; *O. sinensis* grows out of moth larvae. The famous ant-mind-controlling "zombie" species is *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis*, a relative — see §13 [6].

## 13. Fun facts (cited)

1. *Cordyceps militaris* is the **type species of the entire genus *Cordyceps*** — which is exactly why "Ophiocordyceps militaris" is impossible: when the caterpillar fungus *sinensis* was moved to the new genus *Ophiocordyceps* in 2007, *militaris* had to stay behind because a genus is defined by its type species [4].
2. Wild *Ophiocordyceps sinensis* is the most expensive fungus on Earth: on the Tibetan Plateau it provided about **40% of rural households' annual cash income** in 2004, and top-grade material traded for over **US$18,000/kg** in 2008 [7][9].
3. The "zombie fungus" of *The Last of Us* is a real relative — *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* — which infects ants, drives them to clamp onto a leaf vein, and then erupts a stalk from the dead ant's head to rain spores on the colony below [6].
